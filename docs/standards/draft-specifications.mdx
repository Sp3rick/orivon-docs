The draft phase is where every specification begins. A draft is a public
proposal: written, versioned and reviewable, but **not** a promise. All fifteen
specifications in the Orivon series are currently in draft. This page lists
them with their status and points to the full document for each.

<Callout type="warning" title="Everything here is a Draft">
  Drafts describe direction and intent. Nothing on this page is normative or
  binding until it is [Accepted](/docs/standards/accepted-specifications). Do
  not build a production dependency on a draft without tracking its status.
</Callout>

## What every draft page contains

Each draft follows the [template](/docs/standards/specification-template) and
includes:

- **Status**: the lifecycle stage, always Draft for now.
- **Summary**: what the specification defines in a paragraph.
- **Purpose**: why the specification exists.
- **Scope**: what is in and out of the document.
- **Dependencies**: the specifications and systems it builds on.
- **Related specifications**: documents it links to.
- **Implementation status**: how much the reference implementation covers.
- **Future work**: what the draft does not cover yet.

  </Card>
  <Card title="No conformance suites yet">
    The testing framework is defined but has not shipped for any draft.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Moves with the code">
    As the reference implementation changes, drafts are revised and renumbered.
  </Card>

<Callout type="info" title="Draft status policy">
  A draft is updated in place as discussion resolves. When a draft is ready to
  leave the draft phase, it moves through
  [review](/docs/standards/specification-lifecycle) to Accepted with a stable
  1.0.0 version. None have reached that point yet.
</Callout>

## How to read a draft

Reading a draft productively:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check the status">
    Confirm the document is still in draft before relying on it.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the summary and purpose">
    Decide whether the document is relevant before reading the detail.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the detailed specification">
    The normative requirements, written in RFC keyword form.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the considerations">
    Security, privacy, performance and compatibility are part of the contract.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The [Writing Specifications](/docs/standards/writing-specifications) page
explains how to interpret the normative language you will find.

<Callout type="success" title="Contribute">
  Drafts exist to be improved. Discussion happens against each document; see
  [How contributors participate](/docs/standards/overview) to join in.
</Callout>
