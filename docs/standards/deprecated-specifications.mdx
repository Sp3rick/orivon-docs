Deprecation is the honest way to retire a specification. Nothing is deleted and
nothing is silently changed; a deprecated specification stays readable, is
clearly labeled, and points at its replacement. This page documents the
retirement process and tracks its current state.

## Why deprecation exists

Specifications do not last forever. They are superseded by better designs,
replaced by newer standards, or absorbed into another document. The failure
mode to avoid is a specification that stops being recommended but keeps being
implemented, because no one was told.

Deprecation keeps the ecosystem informed so it can move together, at a pace
each consumer can manage.

## The deprecation process

A specification is deprecated through the same
[lifecycle](/docs/standards/specification-lifecycle) discipline as everything
else:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Propose">
    A deprecation proposal explains why the specification is superseded.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Document">
    The deprecation is recorded with its replacement and a timeline.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Mark">
    The specification's status changes to Deprecated in the
    [registry](/docs/standards/specification-registry).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Retire">
    After the migration window, the specification moves to Archived.
  </Step>
</Steps>

A deprecation is never announced and executed in the same release. Consumers
always get a window, per the [migration strategy](/docs/standards/versioning).

## What a deprecation record contains

When a specification is deprecated, its record in the registry and on its page
carries:

<CardGrid columns={2}>
  <Card title="Reason">
    Why the specification is no longer recommended.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Replacement">
    The specification that takes its place, with its identifier and version.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Timeline">
    When the status takes effect and when archival is expected.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Migration path">
    What consumers should do, with references to the replacement's migration
    section.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

The property that makes this safe: **a deprecated specification is still
fully specified.** Consumers mid-migration can keep reading exactly what the
old behavior was.

## Current state

There are no deprecated specifications yet, which is the expected state for a
young program:

| Identifier | Title | Status | Replacement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| - | No deprecated specifications yet | - | - |

Deprecation is a defined process waiting to be used. When a specification is
superseded, it will appear here. The
[versioning](/docs/standards/versioning) and
[lifecycle](/docs/standards/specification-lifecycle) pages define the rules that
will govern it.

<Callout type="info" title="Deprecated versus Archived">
  **Deprecated** means "do not build on this, here is the path forward".
  **Archived** means "historical reference only, outside compliance scope". A
  specification passes through Deprecated before it becomes Archived.
</Callout>
