Maintainers are the **merge authorities** for Orivon repositories and areas.
They are accountable for code quality, release management, and contributor
experience within their scope. Maintainership is earned through demonstrated
contribution and community trust.

## Responsibilities

Every maintainer is responsible for their scope (repository or area):

- Review pull requests in a timely, respectful, and thorough manner.
- Merge changes that meet quality, security, and architectural standards.
- Triage issues: label, prioritize, assign, and close appropriately.
- Manage releases for their scope: versioning, changelogs, publishing.
- Onboard and mentor new contributors.
- Document decisions and rationale for significant changes.
- Coordinate with other maintainers on cross-repo changes.
- Report progress and blockers to the TSC.
- Enforce the Code of Conduct in their scope.
- Participate in security disclosure response when relevant.

## Maintainer types

| Type | Scope | Typical Count |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Repository maintainer** | Single repository (e.g., `orivon-runtime`, `orivon-browser`, `orivon-docs`) | 2-4 per repo |
| **Area maintainer** | Cross-cutting concern (security, CI, accessibility, community, docs) | 1-3 per area |
| **Release maintainer** | Release train execution (stable, beta, nightly) | 2-3 rotating |
| **Emeritus** | Retired maintainers honored for past service | Unlimited |

<Callout type="note" title="Multiple maintainers per scope">
  Every repository has **at least two active maintainers** to ensure bus
  factor, review capacity, and continuity. Single-maintainer repositories
  are flagged as a risk and prioritized for additional appointments.
</Callout>

## Selection process

Maintainership is not applied for — it is **recognized** based on demonstrated
work.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Contribute consistently">
    Build a track record in a specific scope: merged PRs, reliable reviews,
    helpful issue triage, mentoring, documentation improvements.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Demonstrate scope ownership">
    Show sustained responsibility for a repository or area: driving features,
    fixing bugs, improving processes, helping others.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get nominated">
    Existing maintainers or TSC members nominate based on observed contribution.
    Nominations are public with supporting evidence (PRs, reviews, impact).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Community comment period">
    7-day public comment period for the community to support or raise concerns.
  </Step>
  <Step title="TSC appointment">
    TSC reviews nomination and comments. Appointment requires TSC consensus
    (lazy consensus: no objection within 7 days).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Public announcement">
    Appointment announced with scope, rationale, and links to contribution record.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Nomination criteria (typical signals)

| Signal | Weight | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Merged contributions** | High | 20+ merged PRs in scope over 6+ months |
| **Code review quality** | High | 50+ thorough reviews; mentors others |
| **Issue triage** | Medium | Consistent labeling, prioritization, reproduction |
| **Mentoring** | Medium | Onboarded 3+ newcomers; recognized by mentees |
| **Process improvement** | Medium | CI, tooling, docs, standards contributions |
| **Cross-repo impact** | Low (for repo maintainers) | Coordinated changes across repositories |

<Callout type="info" title="No fixed thresholds">
  These are guidelines, not checkboxes. The TSC evaluates the whole picture:
  consistency, quality, community trust, and scope need.
</Callout>

## Expectations

### Responsiveness
- **PR review:** First response within 3 business days for active PRs.
- **Issue triage:** New issues triaged within 2 business days.
- **Communication:** Notify scope partners when unavailable >1 week.

### Quality standards
- Enforce project coding standards, testing requirements, and documentation
  conventions.
- Require security review for capability, crypto, and sandbox changes.
- Block merges that lack tests, docs, or migration plans for breaking changes.

### Community stewardship
- Welcome newcomers; assign good-first-issues; celebrate first merges.
- Mediate technical disagreements constructively.
- Escalate Code of Conduct issues to Community WG promptly.

### Rotation and availability
- **No lifetime tenure.** Maintainers step back when capacity changes.
- **Emeritus transition** is normal and honored — not a demotion.
- **Planned absences** (parental leave, sabbatical, work crunch) are
  supported; temporary co-maintainers appointed if needed.

## Emeritus status

Maintainers who step back (for any reason) are granted **emeritus status**:

- Public acknowledgment of service.
- Retained access to historical context (read-only).
- Invitation to advise on complex issues (no obligation).
- Always welcome to return to active maintainership through the normal process.

<Callout type="note" title="Emeritus is honorific; not operational">
  Emeritus maintainers do not have merge authority and are not counted in
  bus-factor calculations. They are honored contributors, not active
  decision-makers.
</Callout>

## Maintainer vs. TSC vs. Working Group

| Role | Scope | Authority | Selection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Maintainer** | Repository/area | Merge, release, triage | TSC appointment |
| **Working Group Chair** | Cross-cutting topic | Coordinate, recommend | Group + TSC |
| **TSC Member** | Project-wide | Roadmap, standards, escalation | Maintainer election |
