The Whitepapers & Research section is Orivon's long-form technical publication
archive. It collects the reasoning, trade-offs, and long-term vision behind
the platform: everything from formal whitepapers to open engineering questions
that are still being explored.

This library is written for researchers, browser engineers, protocol
designers, security researchers, academic readers, investors, and
contributors who want to understand *why* Orivon is designed the way it is.

## Purpose of the research library

Documentation answers *how to use the platform*. Research answers *why the
platform is the way it is*. The purpose of this library is to:

- Record the architectural decisions behind Orivon and the evidence that
  motivated them.
- Publish exploratory work early, so the ecosystem can participate in open
  research questions.
- Separate stable, production-grade specifications from concepts that are
  still being evaluated.
- Give external researchers a durable, citable archive of the platform's
  technical foundation.

<Note title="Not implementation documentation">
  Research papers are not tutorials and not API references. They describe
  principles, constraints, and trade-offs. For usage guidance see the
  [Developer Documentation](/docs/developers/overview); for exact interfaces
  see the [API Reference](/docs/api-reference/overview).
</Note>

## Documentation vs. research

| Dimension | Documentation | Research |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Question | How do I use or build this? | Why is this the right design? |
| Audience | Developers, operators | Engineers, researchers, investors |
| Stability | Tracks shipped behavior | Includes proposals and open questions |
| Lifecycle | Maintained with releases | Published as versions; superseded by newer papers |
| Status vocabulary | Live, deprecated | Published, draft, experimental, proposal |
| Evidence | Verified against implementation | Arguments, trade-offs, comparisons, references |

The same subject can appear in both places. The [Technical Design](/docs/technical-design/architecture-overview)
level describes the current architecture; the [Architecture Papers](/docs/research/browser-architecture)
in this section analyse the decisions behind that architecture.

## Research methodology

All publications follow a common methodology:

1. **Problem framing.** Every paper begins with a precise statement of the
   problem, including constraints and non-goals.
2. **Evidence review.** Existing work — academic papers, standards, and
   production systems — is surveyed in the [References](/docs/research/references).
3. **Alternatives analysis.** Major options are compared systematically before
   a decision is recorded.
4. **Decision record.** Design documents capture the chosen direction, its
   advantages, and its disadvantages in a durable form.
5. **Status discipline.** Work is labeled `Published`, `Draft`, `Experimental`,
   `Proposal`, or `Concept`. A label is never stronger than the evidence.
6. **Open review.** Papers are versioned and published for community comment
   before being treated as settled.

<Warning title="Status labels are commitments">
  An item labeled `Experimental` or `Proposal` is not production-ready. Do not
  build production integrations on experimental material; build on the
  [Standards](/docs/standards/overview) and [API Reference](/docs/api-reference/overview)
  instead.
</Warning>
