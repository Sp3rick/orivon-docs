Today your identity online is scattered across platforms. Orivon puts identity
back in your hands: one portable identity you control, expressed through open
standards. This page explains how identity works in the ecosystem and why it is
the foundation for everything else.

## What identity means here

An identity is the set of facts that lets someone be recognized and held
accountable online: a name, keys, reputation, credentials and preferences. In
today's web, most of that lives inside platform accounts. In the ecosystem, it
lives in something you own and carry with you.

On the old web, the platforms hold your accounts and decide who you are. In the
ecosystem, you hold an identity and applications ask to work with it. That
reversal of control is the single most important shift the ecosystem makes.

## How identity is represented

Identity is expressed through the **DDoc** standard: a small, signed document
that describes a subject and the keys it controls. The DDoc has a stable
[address](/docs/ecosystem/naming-system) and is stored in decentralized storage,
so it is always retrievable and always verifiable.

Because the DDoc is signed, anyone can verify that a given address really is
controlled by the identity. Because it is stored in decentralized storage, no
single party can take it down.

## Names and resolution

Human readable names are the user friendly face of identity. The
[Naming System](/docs/ecosystem/naming-system) maps names like `alice.orivon` to
the underlying address. Users share names; applications resolve them.

<Callout type="info" title="Did you know?">
  The same resolution mechanism works for applications, modules and
  organizations. Identity is not just for people.
</Callout>

## Verifiable credentials

Beyond the base identity, the ecosystem supports verifiable credentials: signed
statements that an issuer makes about a subject. Examples include:

- A university certifies a degree.
- A service verifies that an account is not a bot.
- An organization issues a role or membership.

Credentials are **presented selectively**. You can prove you are over eighteen
without revealing your date of birth, and you can prove a membership without
revealing the issuer's entire database. The [Security Model](/docs/ecosystem/security-model)
and [Storage](/docs/ecosystem/storage) pages cover how credentials stay safe and
where they live.

## Recovery and portability

Ownership brings responsibility. If you lose the keys, you lose the identity,
so recovery is a first class feature:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Set up recovery">
    The wallet generates recovery materials: a recovery code, split into shares,
    or a trusted recovery contact.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Protect them">
    Recovery materials are kept outside the wallet, in places the wallet cannot
    be compromised from.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Recover">
    Present enough shares or the recovery code, prove control, and derive fresh
    keys that the DDoc is updated to recognize.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Recovery updates the DDoc, so the same address and identity continue to work
after the device is lost.

## Identity and the Web3 Score

Reputation is part of identity. The [Web3 Score](/docs/ecosystem/web3-score) is
one of the signals a user can attach to their identity or consume about others.
A good score is earned through behavior, not bought.
