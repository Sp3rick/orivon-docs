Open source is not just a license choice — it is a governance choice, a
business choice, and a values choice. This page explains why Orivon is open
source, what that means in practice, and how it shapes the project.

## The license: Apache 2.0

Orivon is licensed under the **Apache License 2.0**.

<Callout type="info" title="License file">
  The full license text is in the repository root: [LICENSE](/LICENSE).
  It is a permissive license that grants patent rights, allows commercial use,
  and requires attribution and preservation of notices.
</Callout>

### Why Apache 2.0?

| Factor | Why Apache 2.0 fits |
| --- | --- |
| **Permissive** | Anyone can use, modify, distribute, and sell Orivon — including in proprietary products. |
| **Patent grant** | Contributors grant patent rights, protecting users and downstream adopters. |
| **Attribution** | Credit is required, preserving the project's identity. |
| **No viral effect** | Unlike copyleft licenses, Apache 2.0 does not force derivative works to be open source. |
| **Enterprise-friendly** | Widely understood and accepted by corporate legal teams. |
| **Foundation-compatible** | The license used by the Apache Software Foundation and many neutral foundations. |

<Warning title="License is not governance">
  The license governs *copyright and patent rights*. It does not govern *how
  the project makes decisions*. Governance is a separate, community-owned
  process documented in [Governance](/docs/community/governance).
</Warning>

## What open source means for Orivon

### Source is open by default

All code, documentation, standards, and research are developed in public
repositories. There is no private development track.

### Process is open by default

- Discussions happen in public forums.
- Decisions are recorded in public decision logs.
- Roadmaps are published and updated in the open.
- Meetings are scheduled on public calendars; minutes are published.

### Governance is community-owned

The project is not owned by a single company. It is governed by contributors
through the model in [Governance](/docs/community/governance). The license
permits forks; the governance model makes forks unnecessary by giving the
community a voice.

### Community over vendors

No vendor — not even the original authors — has special rights. Influence
comes from contribution, not from employment or sponsorship.

## What open source does NOT mean

| Myth | Reality |
| --- | --- |
| "Anyone can push to main" | All changes go through review; maintainers merge. |
| "No one is accountable" | Maintainers and the TSC are accountable for quality and direction. |
| "Free labor" | Contributors choose to contribute; the project recognizes and rewards them. |
| "No business model" | Companies build businesses *on* Orivon; the project benefits from the ecosystem. |
| "Chaos" | The project has rigorous processes: review, testing, security, release. |

## Why this matters for you

| If you are… | This means… |
| --- | --- |
| A **user** | You can inspect, modify, and self-host the browser. No vendor lock-in. |
| A **developer** | You can build on the platform without asking permission or paying royalties. |
| A **company** | You can embed Orivon in products, offer services, and contribute back. |
| A **researcher** | You can cite, extend, and challenge the published research. |
| A **contributor** | Your work is visible, credited, and governs the project's direction. |
