Working groups are the community's way of organizing focused, cross-cutting
work. Each group owns a topic — not a repository — and coordinates the people
and projects touching that topic across the ecosystem.

## What working groups do

Working groups exist to:

- Coordinate work across repositories and teams.
- Build consensus on their topic and make recommendations to the
  [Technical Steering Committee](/docs/community/technical-steering-committee).
- Maintain topic documentation, guidelines, and checklists.
- Onboard and mentor people interested in the topic.

<Note title="Groups coordinate, maintainers own">
  Working groups do not replace [maintainers](/docs/community/maintainers).
  Maintainers own specific repositories; working groups coordinate topics
  across repositories. The two roles overlap and cooperate.
</Note>

## How working groups operate

Each working group follows a common operating model:

- **Chair.** Each group has a chair who runs meetings and tracks action items.
- **Open meetings.** Meetings are scheduled on public calendars and minutes
  are published.
- **Decision record.** Groups publish recommendations to the TSC through
  [decision proposals](/docs/community/decision-making).
- **Charter review.** Charters are reviewed periodically by the TSC.

## Joining a working group

Anyone can participate. To join:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick a group">
    Read the group charters and find the topic you care about.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Introduce yourself">
    Introduce yourself in the group's public discussion and attend a meeting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Contribute">
    Pick up an open item from the group's backlog. Consistent contributions
    grow into membership.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Lead">
    Experienced members may volunteer as chairs or propose new groups to the
    TSC.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="success" title="No invitation required">
  Working groups are open. You do not need anyone's permission to attend a
  meeting or pick up an item — show up and start contributing.
</Callout>

## Proposing a new working group

To propose a new group, submit a [decision proposal](/docs/community/decision-making)
including:

1. The topic and why it needs cross-cutting coordination.
2. An initial set of goals and deliverables.
3. The expected participants.

The TSC reviews and ratifies the charter, then the group starts.
