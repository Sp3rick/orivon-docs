The **Technical Steering Committee (TSC)** is the project's highest technical
authority. It sets technical direction, approves standards, and resolves
stalemates that working groups and maintainers cannot.

## Purpose

The TSC exists to:

- Guard the project's long-term technical coherence.
- Approve standards and architectural decisions with ecosystem-wide impact.
- Resolve cross-cutting disagreements that working groups cannot settle.
- Represent the project's technical voice to the community and partners.

<Callout type="info" title="TSC vs. Foundation">
  The TSC is a *technical* body elected by contributors. The Orivon Foundation
  (planned) is a *legal and financial* steward. They coordinate but have
  separate mandates. See [Ecosystem Governance](/docs/ecosystem/governance).
</Callout>

## Composition

| Aspect | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Size** | 7–11 members (odd number for voting). |
| **Term** | 1 year, renewable. |
| **Eligibility** | Active maintainers with ≥1 year of sustained contribution. |
| **Election** | Annual, by the maintainer electorate. |
| **Chair** | Elected by TSC members from within the committee. |

The current roster is published on the project site and updated after each
election.

## Responsibilities

The TSC is accountable for:

<CheckList
  items={[
    'Technical roadmap and release strategy.',
    'Standards ratification and versioning.',
    'Architecture decisions with cross-subsystem impact.',
    'Maintainer appointments and emeritus status.',
    'Working group charter approval and periodic review.',
    'Resolution of escalated disputes (see Decision Making).',
    'Public meeting minutes and decision records.',
  ]}
/>

The TSC does **not**:

- Manage day-to-day repository operations (that is maintainers' scope).
- Allocate funding or legal assets (that is the Foundation's scope).
- Decide ecosystem content policy (that is ecosystem governance).

## Meetings and transparency

- **Cadence:** Bi-weekly, public calendar.
- **Attendance:** Open to all contributors; only members vote.
- **Minutes:** Published within 48 hours.
- **Agenda:** Published 1 week in advance; community may propose items.

## Decision authority

The TSC uses **lazy consensus** for most decisions: a proposal is accepted if
no member objects within the review period. For foundational decisions
(governance changes, licensing, standards process), the TSC requires a
**supermajority** (≥2/3) recorded vote.

Voting details are on the [Decision Making](/docs/community/decision-making)
page.

## Current TSC

The current TSC members, their affiliations, and their terms are published on
the project site. The roster is updated after each election and any interim
appointment.

<Callout type="success" title="The TSC is accountable to the community">
  TSC members are elected by maintainers. If the community believes the TSC
  is not serving the project, the election process is the mechanism for
  change. Governance belongs to the community.
</Callout>
