The community is the group of people who build, test, document, govern, and
champion Orivon. It is distributed by design: there is no headquarters, and
anyone can participate from anywhere.

This page describes how the community is structured, where conversations
happen, and how to get involved.

## Community structure

The community has a few concentric circles. Everyone starts at the edge and
moves inward through demonstrated contribution.

<Flow>
  <DiagramNode title="Users" description="Browse, test, give feedback, report issues." />
  <FlowArrowDown />
  <DiagramNode title="Contributors" description="Fix issues, write docs, review PRs." />
  <FlowArrowDown />
  <DiagramNode title="Maintainers" description="Own repositories and review processes." />
  <FlowArrowDown />
  <DiagramNode title="Leaders" description="TSC members, working group chairs, and program leads." />

Movement between circles is driven by contribution, not election campaigns or
tenure. The paths are documented in the [Contributor Guide](/docs/contributors/overview)
and [Governance](/docs/community/governance).

## Communication channels

| Channel | Purpose | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Discussion forum | Long-form technical and community discussion | RFCs, proposals, design questions |
| Issue tracker | Bugs, feature requests, and tasks | Concrete, actionable work |
| Real-time chat | Quick questions and coordination | Community chatter, meeting pings |
| Mailing list | Announcements and official decisions | Formal notices, releases |
| Blog & newsletter | News and updates | Followers who want the highlights |

<Note title="Official channels only">
  All official announcements happen through the channels listed above. Orivon
  contributors will never message you asking for tokens, keys, or payments.
  Be wary of impersonators anywhere else.
</Note>

## Discussions and forums

Technical discussion happens on the discussion forum, where proposals are
workshopped before they become formal [decision proposals](/docs/community/decision-making).

Common discussion categories:

- **Ideas** — feature and design brainstorms.
- **RFCs** — request-for-comment threads for significant changes.
- **Design Q&A** — questions about the architecture and standards.
- **Ecosystem** — coordination between ecosystem projects.
- **Community** — meta-topics about how the community runs.

Discussions are public and archived. Search before posting; the answer may
already exist.

## Events and meetups

The community gathers in person and online through:

- **Meetups** — regional, casual, recurring gatherings. See [Meetups](/docs/community/meetups).
- **Community events** — talks, workshops, and summits. See [Community Events](/docs/community/community-events).
- **Hackathons** — focused build sprints with prizes. See [Hackathons](/docs/community/hackathons).

If there is no meetup near you, the community provides guidance for [starting
your own](/docs/community/meetups#starting-a-new-meetup).

## Recognition

Contributions are recognized publicly through the [Contribution Recognition](/docs/community/contribution-recognition)
program: contributor levels, maintainer appointment, release credits, and
ecosystem showcase spotlights.

The recognition system is designed to be:

- **Merit-based** — based on visible, verifiable contributions.
- **Public** — records are open to the community.
- **Non-exclusive** — recognition in one area never blocks another.

## Community guidelines

All participation is governed by the [Code of Conduct](/docs/contributors/code-of-conduct),
which applies to every channel, event, and discussion — online and offline.
