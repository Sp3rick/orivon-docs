Every significant decision in Orivon follows a documented process. This page
describes that process: how proposals work, when voting happens, and how
disagreements are resolved.

## Decision tiers

Not all decisions are equal. The project uses three tiers:

| Tier | Scope | Examples | Process |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Operational** | Day-to-day | Merging a PR, triaging an issue | Maintainer discretion |
| **Project** | Feature, policy, process | Adding a feature, changing release cadence | Decision proposal |
| **Foundational** | Governance, licensing, standards | Changing governance, licensing, standards process | Decision proposal + vote |

<Callout type="note" title="When in doubt, propose">
  If you are not sure whether a decision needs a proposal, open one. The cost
  of a lightweight proposal is low; the cost of an undocumented decision is
  high.
</Callout>

## The decision proposal process

All project and foundational decisions go through a **decision proposal**
(also called an RFC or design doc):

<Steps>
  <Step title="Draft">
    The author writes a proposal with: problem statement, proposed solution,
    alternatives considered, risks, and migration plan. Use the proposal
    template.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open for comment">
    The proposal is posted to the discussion forum with the `proposal` tag.
    Minimum comment period: 14 days for project decisions, 30 days for
    foundational decisions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Discuss and iterate">
    The community comments. The author updates the proposal in response.
    Discussion is public and threaded.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Call for consensus">
    When discussion settles, the author (or a maintainer/TSC member) calls
    for consensus. This starts the voting or lazy-consensus window.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decide">
    - **Project decisions:** Lazy consensus. If no TSC member objects within
      7 days, the proposal is accepted.
    - **Foundational decisions:** Recorded vote. Supermajority (≥2/3) of
      eligible TSC members required.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Record">
    The outcome (accepted, rejected, deferred) is recorded in the decision
    log with the final proposal, vote counts (if any), and rationale.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Lazy consensus

For project decisions, the project uses **lazy consensus**:

- The proposal is accepted if no TSC member objects within 7 days.
- An objection must include a rationale and a suggested alternative.
- If an objection is raised, the proposal moves to discussion and may be
  revised or escalated to a vote.

This balances speed with accountability: silence is consent, but any member
can force a deeper review.

## Voting

### Eligibility

Voting eligibility is tied to contribution, not to affiliation or payment:

- **TSC members** vote on foundational decisions.
- **Maintainers** elect the TSC.
- **Working group chairs** may be consulted but do not vote unless also TSC
  members.

### Vote mechanics

| Decision type | Electorate | Threshold | Period |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TSC election | Maintainers | Simple majority | 14 days |
| Foundational decision | TSC members | Supermajority (≥2/3) | 14 days |
| Standards ratification | TSC members | Supermajority (≥2/3) | 14 days |
| Governance amendment | TSC members | Supermajority (≥2/3) | 30 days |

Votes are **public**: individual votes and the final tally are published.
Abstentions are recorded and do not count toward the threshold.

### Tie-breaking

In the event of a tie in a TSC election, a runoff is held between the tied
candidates within 7 days.

## Appeals

Anyone may appeal a decision within 30 days of its recording:

<Steps>
  <Step title="File appeal">
    Submit a written appeal to the TSC with new evidence, a procedural error
    claim, or a conflict-of-interest claim.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review">
    The TSC reviews the appeal in a public meeting. The original decision
    stands unless the TSC votes to overturn (supermajority).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Final">
    The appeal outcome is final and recorded. No further appeal within the
    project.
  </Step>
</Steps>

For ecosystem-level disputes (naming, content), see
[Ecosystem Governance](/docs/ecosystem/governance).

## Decision records

Every accepted proposal becomes a **decision record** (ADR-style):

- Immutable once recorded.
- Stored in the project's decision log.
- Includes: title, status, date, decision makers, rationale, alternatives,
  vote count (if any), and links to discussion.

Decision records are the project's institutional memory. Search them before
proposing something that may have been decided.

## Quick reference: when to use what

| Situation | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Fix a bug, add a test | Open a PR (operational) |
| Add a feature, change a config default | Decision proposal (project) |
| Change release process, add a dependency policy | Decision proposal (project) |
| Change governance, license, standards process | Decision proposal (foundational) |
| Ratify a standard | Decision proposal (foundational) |
| Elect TSC | Election (maintainers vote) |
| Disagree with a TSC decision | Appeal (within 30 days) |

<Callout type="success" title="The process is yours">
  The decision process is not a wall — it is a tool. If it feels heavy for a
  particular decision, propose a lighter process for that class of decisions.
  The community owns the process.
</Callout>
