Recognition is how the community says "this work matters." It is not a
replacement for compensation — it is a public record of who did what, and a
path from first contribution to project leadership.

## Why recognition matters

- **Visibility.** Contributors see that their work is noticed.
- **Progression.** Recognition maps the path from newcomer to leader.
- **Trust.** Public records let the community evaluate experience.
- **Motivation.** Celebration sustains long-term participation.

<Callout type="info" title="Recognition ≠ compensation">
  Recognition is public credit. The [Grants Program](/docs/community/grants-program)
  and ecosystem opportunities provide financial support. They are separate
  systems that work together.
</Callout>

## Contributor levels

Every contributor has a **level** based on their cumulative, verified
contributions. Levels are automatically calculated from the public record.

| Level | Criteria (typical) | Recognition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Newcomer** | First merged PR | Welcome badge, onboarding mentor |
| **Contributor** | 5+ merged PRs, 3+ months active | Contributor badge, forum flair |
| **Active Contributor** | 20+ merged PRs, reviews, 6+ months | Active badge, invited to working groups |
| **Core Contributor** | 50+ merged PRs, cross-repo impact, mentoring | Core badge, release credits, TSC electorate |
| **Maintainer** | Appointed per [Maintainers](/docs/community/maintainers) | Maintainer badge, repository ownership |

Levels are not ranks — they are **signals**. A Core Contributor is not "better
than" a Contributor; they have a different scope of demonstrated impact.

## How contributions are counted

The system counts **verified, public contributions**:

- Merged pull requests (code, docs, tests, tooling).
- Code reviews on merged PRs.
- Issues triaged and labeled.
- Discussion forum answers marked helpful.
- Event organization (meetups, hackathons, workshops).
- Working group participation (attendance, action items).

<Note title="Quality over quantity">
  One thoughtful architectural review counts more than ten trivial typo fixes.
  The system weights contributions by type and impact, not just raw count.
</Note>

## Public contribution record

Every contributor has a **public profile** showing:

- Current level and progress to next level.
- Contribution history (repos, types, timeline).
- Recognitions earned (badges, showcase features, release credits).
- Working group and program participation.

Profiles are generated from the public Git history and forum data. No private
data is included.

## Recognition programs

### Release credits

Every stable release includes a **credits section** listing contributors by
level. Core Contributors and Maintainers are listed by name; Contributors and
Active Contributors are listed in aggregate.

### Community showcase

The [Community Showcase](/docs/community/community-showcase) features a
monthly spotlight on a contributor or ecosystem project. Nominations are open
to the community.

### Maintainer appointment

Becoming a maintainer is the highest recognition of technical trust. It is
earned through the process in [Maintainers](/docs/community/maintainers) and
announced publicly.

### Conference and travel support

Active Contributors and above may apply for travel support to present Orivon
work at conferences. See the [Community Roadmap](/docs/community/community-roadmap)
for the program status.

## Earning recognition

<Steps>
  <Step title="Start">
    Follow the [Contributor Guide](/docs/contributors/overview). Your first
    merged PR earns the Newcomer level.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Consistency">
    Regular contributions — code, review, docs, community — build toward
    Contributor and Active Contributor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Impact">
    Cross-repo work, mentoring, and architectural contributions build toward
    Core Contributor.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Leadership">
    Sustained impact and community trust lead to maintainer nomination.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Recognition is revocable

Recognition reflects *current* standing. If a contributor is found to have
violated the [Code of Conduct](/docs/contributors/code-of-conduct) or
misrepresented contributions, recognitions may be revoked through the same
transparent process that grants them.

<Callout type="success" title="The record belongs to you">
  Your contribution record is public, portable, and yours. It travels with
  you — whether you stay in Orivon, move to another project, or build a
  career on the experience.
</Callout>
