Maintainers are the people accountable for the day-to-day health of Orivon's
repositories and areas. They review pull requests, manage releases, onboard
contributors, and protect the quality of the codebase. Maintainership is a
service role earned through contribution.

## What a maintainer is

A maintainer owns a **defined scope**: a repository (for example the runtime,
the browser, or the documentation site) or a cross-cutting area (for example
security review). Within that scope, maintainers are the point of accountability:

- They review and merge pull requests.
- They triage and prioritize issues.
- They manage releases for their scope.
- They guide and mentor contributors.
- They report progress to the [Technical Steering Committee](/docs/community/technical-steering-committee).

<Note title="Ownership, not dictatorship">
  Maintainers own *accountability*, not the code. They make decisions in the
  open, consider community input, and follow the [decision-making process](/docs/community/decision-making)
  for anything significant.
</Note>

## Becoming a maintainer

Maintainership is earned through consistent, high-quality contribution.
There is no application form and no payment required.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Contribute">
    Start with the [Contributor Guide](/docs/contributors/overview). Build a
    track record of merged pull requests, reliable reviews, and helpful
    participation.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get noticed">
    Active contributors are recognized through the [Contribution Recognition](/docs/community/contribution-recognition)
    program and community showcase.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Nominate">
    A contributor may be nominated by existing maintainers or the community
    when they show sustained, trustworthy work in a scope.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Appoint">
    Appointments are confirmed by the relevant maintainers and the Technical
    Steering Committee, and announced publicly.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### Nomination criteria

Typical signals considered at nomination:

| Signal | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Consistent merged contributions | Demonstrated ability and commitment |
| Reliable code review | The project scales on good review, not just code |
| Constructive community participation | The project values how people treat each other |
| Understanding of project processes | Releases, security, and docs conventions |
| Mentoring behavior | Growing the next generation of contributors |

There is no fixed quota and no time requirement; readiness is judged by the
community on the evidence.

## Maintainer expectations

Maintainers are expected to be **responsive but not omniscient**. Reasonable
expectations include:

- Responding to pings and review requests within a few days.
- Communicating when unavailable (vacation, work load, life events).
- Asking for help when a decision is outside their expertise.

<Warning title="Maintainers are not on-call heroes">
  A maintainer's health and personal life come first. If a maintainer is
  unavailable, the project's [bus-factor](/docs/community/governance) protections
  — multiple reviewers, documented processes, and rotating responsibilities —
  keep the project moving without pressure on any one person.
</Warning>

## Maintainer types

| Type | Scope | Typical duties |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repository maintainer | A single repository | Review, merge, release that repo |
| Area maintainer | A cross-cutting area | Security, docs, CI, accessibility, community |
| Release maintainer | The release train | Cut versions, write release notes, coordinate |
| Emeritus | Retired maintainers | Advisory, historical knowledge, no obligation |

### Emeritus status

Maintainers who step back for any reason are honored as **emeritus** — their
work is credited, their access is retired gracefully, and they are always
welcome back. This keeps the project respectful of the humans who built it.

## Reviewing work

A large part of maintainership is review. Maintainers follow the project's
review standards (see the [Code Review guide](/docs/contributors/code-review))
and are expected to model what good review looks like: thorough, kind, and
actionable.

## Current maintainers

The roster of current maintainers and their scopes is maintained on the
project. As with all governance records, the list is public and updated as
appointments change.
