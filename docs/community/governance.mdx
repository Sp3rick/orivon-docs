Governance is how the project makes decisions and distributes power. This page
describes the *project-level* governance model: who holds responsibility for
what, how decisions are made, and how the process stays accountable.

<Note title="Project governance vs. ecosystem governance">
  This page is about how the Orivon *project* is run. The wider *ecosystem* —
  standards, naming disputes, content rules, and the Orivon Foundation — is
  governed separately. See [Ecosystem Governance](/docs/ecosystem/governance)
  for that model. The two models share values and overlap at the standards
  layer, but they are distinct.
</Note>

## Governance model

Orivon uses a **meritocratic, layered model** inspired by the Apache
Software Foundation. Power is derived from demonstrated contribution rather
than from employment, appointment, or money.

The layers, from widest to most focused:

| Body | Scope | Appointed by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Community](/docs/community/community) | Discussion, proposals, review | Self-selected |
| [Working Groups](/docs/community/working-groups) | Cross-cutting technical topics | Community members |
| [Maintainers](/docs/community/maintainers) | Individual repositories and areas | Committers, TSC |
| [Technical Steering Committee](/docs/community/technical-steering-committee) | Technical direction and standards | Maintainers, community |

<Callout type="info" title="Why Apache-style?">
  Apache-style governance is a proven way to run an open source project at
  scale: it is transparent, it distributes power, and it lets contributors
  advance based on demonstrated work rather than corporate sponsorship. It
  matches Orivon's mission of returning control of the ecosystem to its
  community.
</Callout>

## Responsibilities

Each body has a clear scope of responsibility:

- **Community** proposes, discusses, and reviews. It is the source of all
  legitimate authority.
- **Working groups** coordinate cross-cutting work (security, docs, research,
  and more) and make recommendations to the TSC.
- **Maintainers** are accountable for the quality and health of specific
  repositories: reviewing pull requests, managing releases, and guiding
  contributors.
- **The TSC** is accountable for overall technical direction, the release
  process, and standards decisions.

<Warning title="No single point of control">
  No individual — and no single company or organization — has unilateral
  authority over the project. Significant decisions require the TSC, and the
  most significant decisions require community review.
</Warning>

## Leadership

Leadership roles are earned through contribution:

| Role | Responsibility | Path |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contributor | Proposes and reviews work | Any community member |
| Working group member | Active, consistent participation in a group | Invitation or application |
| Maintainer | Owns a repository's health | Appointment after demonstrated contributions |
| TSC member | Sets technical direction | Nomination and election |

None of these roles is permanent. Leadership is a service role, and it is
revocable through the same transparent processes that grant it. See
[Maintainers](/docs/community/maintainers) and
[Technical Steering Committee](/docs/community/technical-steering-committee).

## Decision-making

Decisions fall into three tiers, each with a different process:

| Tier | Examples | Process |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Operational** | Merging a bugfix PR, triaging an issue | Maintainer discretion |
| **Project** | Adding a feature, changing release policy | [Decision proposal](/docs/community/decision-making) |
| **Foundational** | Changing governance, licensing, standards | Decision proposal + community review |

Every decision leaves a public record: an issue, a pull request, or a decision
document. Nothing of consequence happens silently. The full process — lazy
consensus, voting thresholds, and appeals — is on the
[Decision Making](/docs/community/decision-making) page.

## Voting

Voting is used for significant decisions: TSC elections, governance changes,
and (for the ecosystem) standards ratification.

Principles:

- **Eligibility is tied to contribution**, not to affiliation or payment.
- **Votes are recorded and public.** Vote counts and identities are published.
- **Abstentions are respected.** Members may abstain without penalty.
- **Thresholds are documented** for each decision type and applied consistently.

See [Voting in Decision Making](/docs/community/decision-making#voting).

## Transparency

The governance model depends on trust, and trust depends on transparency. The
project commits to:

<CheckList
  items={[
    'Public meeting minutes for the TSC and working groups.',
    'Public decision records with rationale and alternatives.',
    'Public maintainer appointments and role changes.',
    'Public release notes, budgets, and program updates.',
    'A public appeal process for contested decisions.',
  ]}
/>

## Conflict resolution

Disagreement is normal and healthy. The project's approach:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Discuss">
    Disagreements are first discussed in the open. Most conflicts resolve
    through evidence and debate.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Escalate">
    If no consensus emerges, the issue escalates to the relevant working group
    or maintainers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="TSC review">
    Stalemates reach the Technical Steering Committee, which makes a recorded
    decision.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Appeal">
    Anyone may appeal a TSC decision through the formal appeals process in
    [Decision Making](/docs/community/decision-making).
  </Step>
</Steps>

For disputes inside the wider ecosystem (naming, content, namespace policy),
see [Ecosystem Governance](/docs/ecosystem/governance).

## Amending governance

Governance is not immutable, but it changes deliberately. Amendments follow
the **foundational** decision tier: a formal proposal, a comment period, a
recorded vote, and a review window before adoption.

<Callout type="success" title="Governance belongs to the community">
  Governance is a living document. If you believe the model can be fairer or
  more effective, the [proposal process](/docs/community/decision-making) is
  open to you.
</Callout>
